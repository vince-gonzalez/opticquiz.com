About the flicker fusion test

A dot flickers between black and bright. Slow, you see it blink; fast enough, the blinking fuses into a steady glow. The frequency where flicker becomes steady is your critical flicker fusion (CFF) threshold — a genuine measure of how your visual system handles change over time.

There's a hard physical limit worth understanding: a display can only flicker up to half its refresh rate, so a 60 Hz screen caps this test around 30 Hz. We probe your display's refresh and disclose the ceiling; if you reach it, your true threshold is simply higher than the screen can show. CFF also rises with brighter light and a larger stimulus and falls when you're tired, so it's a fun perceptual measure, not a medical test.

Frequently asked questions

What is critical flicker fusion?
The frequency at which a flickering light stops looking like it's blinking and appears steady. It reflects the temporal resolution of your visual system.
What is a normal flicker fusion threshold?
Roughly 20 to 40 Hz for most people, rising with brightness and stimulus size and falling with fatigue. There's a wide normal range.
Why is the test capped by my screen?
A screen can only flicker up to half its refresh rate, so a 60 Hz display caps the test near 30 Hz. We detect your refresh and tell you the ceiling.
Does flicker fusion mean anything about my health?
It's a fun perceptual measure that shifts with lighting, stimulus size, and tiredness. It is not a medical or diagnostic test.
