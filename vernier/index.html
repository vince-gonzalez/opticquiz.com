About the vernier acuity test

Vernier acuity is a hyperacuity — the ability to detect that two line segments are misaligned, at a scale finer than a single cone in your retina. This test shows two short vertical lines with the lower one nudged a hair left or right; you call the direction, and a two-alternative staircase narrows the offset until you reach your limit.

The pixel offset at your threshold is measured directly; the arc-second figure is estimated from the viewing distance you enter and an assumed screen pixel size, so it's approximate. What makes sub-pixel offsets possible at all is anti-aliasing, which lets the browser draw a line between pixels — your visual system then averages across receptors to resolve alignment below the pixel grid. It's a genuinely different measure from letter-chart sharpness.

Frequently asked questions

What is vernier acuity?
The ability to detect a small misalignment between line segments. It's called a hyperacuity because people resolve offsets far finer than the spacing of the cones sampling the image.
How is it different from 20/20 acuity?
Letter acuity measures the smallest detail you can resolve; vernier measures the smallest offset you can detect — typically many times finer.
Are the arc-second numbers exact?
The pixel threshold is measured directly, but the arc-second conversion depends on the distance you entered and an assumed pixel size, so treat it as an estimate.
How can I detect an offset smaller than one pixel?
Anti-aliasing renders lines at sub-pixel positions, and your visual system averages across many receptors, letting you resolve alignment below the physical pixel spacing.
