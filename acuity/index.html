About this visual acuity test

This is a real tumbling-E eye chart that runs in your browser — and unlike most online eye tests, the letters are rendered true-to-size. A 20/20 optotype is defined by physics: its critical detail subtends 5 arc-minutes of visual angle. To honor that on your screen, you first calibrate with any bank card (a globally standard 85.6 mm wide), which tells us your display's true pixels-per-millimeter. Combined with your viewing distance, every E is drawn at a genuinely correct visual angle.

You identify which way each E's prongs point, one eye at a time, down a level ladder from 20/200 toward 20/16. Wear your usual glasses or contacts — the result reflects your corrected vision. Two honest limits apply: very small optotypes hit your screen's pixel floor at close range (we compute and show that limit rather than fake it), and this estimates functional sharpness — it is not a refraction and cannot produce a prescription.

Frequently asked questions

Can you test visual acuity online accurately?
A useful estimate, yes — if letters are calibrated to real size (we use a credit card) and your distance is known. It's not a clinical exam or a prescription.
Why do I need a credit card for the eye test?
Screens vary in pixel density, so a card — a standard 85.6 mm everywhere — acts as a ruler to make the letters true-to-size at your screen's real scale.
What is the tumbling E test?
An eye chart of the letter E rotated four ways; you report the direction. It measures acuity with no reading needed, so it works for any language or young children.
Does this replace an eye exam?
No — it estimates current sharpness at a set distance, but can't refract, detect disease, or prescribe. A surprising result is a reason to book an exam.
