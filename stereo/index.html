PICK A MODE

How it works

You'll see a field of random dots divided into four quadrants. In one quadrant, a square floats in front of the background. Say which quadrant it's in. Each time you're right the depth shrinks; the test ends after two misses at the same depth.

Sit about an arm's length from the screen and keep your head level — tilting breaks the effect.

ABOUT THIS TEST

A random-dot stereogram contains no shape that either eye can see by itself. The dots are random; a region of them is shifted sideways between the left and right images. Only when the brain fuses the two views does the shape appear, floating in front of the noise. Béla Julesz introduced the method in 1960 precisely to remove monocular cues — outline, shading, size — so that what remains is stereopsis alone.

Can a computer screen measure stereo vision?

Only partly. A flat screen shows both eyes the same image, so depth has to be created artificially — with red-cyan anaglyph glasses, or by free-fusing two side-by-side panels. Without one of those, no browser test can measure stereopsis at all. This test screens for whether gross stereopsis is present; it cannot produce a clinical stereoacuity value in arc seconds the way a Titmus, TNO or Randot test can.

What is a random-dot stereogram?

A field of random dots that contains no visible shape to either eye alone. A region of the dots is shifted horizontally between the left and right images, so the shape only appears when the brain fuses the two views. It was introduced by Béla Julesz in 1960 and is the standard way to test stereopsis without monocular cues — you cannot pass it by guessing from one eye.

Why did I fail if my eyes are fine?

Failing this screen does not necessarily mean absent stereopsis. In free-fusion mode, many people simply cannot free-fuse, which is a learned skill, not a vision defect. In anaglyph mode, poor-quality or wrongly-oriented red-cyan glasses, screen colour calibration, and low display brightness all interfere. A real result requires a clinician with calibrated targets.

Do I need red-cyan glasses?

For the anaglyph mode, yes — standard red-left, cyan-right 3D glasses. If you do not have them, the free-fusion mode shows two panels side by side that you can fuse by crossing or diverging your eyes, though that mode is harder and its failures are less meaningful.

What does the number mean?

The result is the smallest horizontal disparity, in screen pixels, at which you could still locate the shape. Converting pixels to arc seconds requires knowing your exact pixel pitch and viewing distance, neither of which a web page can measure — so this test reports pixels and stops there rather than inventing a precision it does not have.

Not a diagnosis. If you have double vision, a turned eye, or a sudden change in depth perception, see an optometrist or ophthalmologist. Children in particular should be checked in person, since untreated amblyopia is time-sensitive.
