How Far Should You Sit From Your Screen?

For a computer monitor, sit about an arm’s length away — roughly 50 to 70 cm — with the top of the screen at or just below eye level so your gaze tilts slightly down. To ease eye strain, follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Viewing distance also matters for any on-screen vision test, since it sets how large letters appear to your eye.

The comfortable setup

A good default is an arm’s length from a laptop or monitor (about 50–70 cm), a bit farther for a large screen, with the top of the display at or just below eye level. Phones are naturally closer, around 30–40 cm. There’s nothing magic about the exact number — comfort and a slightly downward gaze angle matter more than precision.

“Computer eye strain” (digital eye strain) comes mostly from reduced blinking, glare, and long unbroken focus, not from the screen damaging your eyes. The fixes are boring and effective: the 20-20-20 rule, decent lighting to cut glare, and blinking on purpose.

Why distance matters for a vision test

A letter’s difficulty depends on the angle it makes at your eye, which depends on both its physical size and how far away you are. That’s why a trustworthy on-screen visual acuity test asks for your viewing distance and calibrates letter sizes to match — sit closer and letters are easier, farther and they’re harder. Getting the distance right is what makes the result meaningful.

Frequently asked questions

How far should I sit from my monitor?
About an arm’s length — roughly 50 to 70 cm — with the top of the screen at or just below eye level, and a little farther for large displays.
What is the 20-20-20 rule?
Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds. It relaxes your focusing muscles and reduces digital eye strain.
Does screen time permanently damage your eyes?
There’s no strong evidence that normal screen use permanently damages adult eyes. It commonly causes temporary eye strain from reduced blinking, glare, and sustained focus.
Why do my eyes hurt after looking at screens?
Usually digital eye strain: you blink less, focus at one distance for long stretches, and battle glare. Breaks, lighting, and the 20-20-20 rule help.
