How to Test for Astigmatism at Home

To screen for astigmatism at home, look at a fan or dial of radial lines pointing in all directions, one eye at a time. If some line directions look darker, sharper, or bolder than the ones perpendicular to them, that suggests astigmatism along that axis. A home test can hint at astigmatism and its rough orientation, but it can’t measure your prescription — only an optometrist’s refraction can.

Why the fan test works

Astigmatism means the cornea or lens is shaped more like a rugby ball than a sphere, so the eye focuses one orientation more sharply than the one at right angles to it. On a radial-line fan, that shows up directly: lines along the sharply-focused meridian look crisper and darker, while the perpendicular lines look fainter or blurred.

Which lines stand out points to your astigmatic axis — the orientation your eye focuses best. Test each eye separately, since astigmatism can differ between them.

What a home test can and can’t do

A home fan test is a screening suggestion only. It can raise a flag and hint at the axis, but it can’t measure how much astigmatism you have or produce a prescription — that needs an optometrist’s refraction with proper instruments.

Try it both with and without your usual glasses or contacts. If the lines look even with your correction on, good; if a direction still stands out, that can hint at under-correction worth mentioning at your next exam. Astigmatism is extremely common and easily corrected, so a positive screen is a reason to book an eye test, not to worry.

Frequently asked questions

How do you check for astigmatism at home?
View a fan of radial lines one eye at a time. If some directions look darker or sharper than the perpendicular ones, it suggests astigmatism along that axis.
What does astigmatism look like?
Vision can be blurred or slightly doubled at all distances, with lights streaking or flaring at night. On a line fan, some orientations look sharper and darker than others.
Can astigmatism go away on its own?
Astigmatism usually stays fairly stable and doesn’t simply disappear, though the amount can change over time. It’s easily corrected with glasses, contacts, or surgery.
Is astigmatism serious?
It’s very common and rarely serious — mostly it just needs correcting. A large or changing astigmatism should be checked by an eye-care professional.
